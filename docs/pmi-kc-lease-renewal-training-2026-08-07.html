Priority 1 30 minutes

One renewal, start to finish

The renewal workspace moves through four stages in order. You cannot skip ahead, because each stage needs a decision from the one before it. We will walk one lease all the way through together, then you will have three more to do on your own.

Where it lives: Lease Renewal, then the live renewal desk.

1

Data check

The app reads the lease from RentVine and the matching row from the renewal spreadsheet, then puts them side by side. Where the two disagree, it says so rather than picking one.

Look for: the lease end date, the current rent, and any disagreement raised between the two sources. A raised disagreement is the app working correctly, not a fault.

2

Owner decision

You record what the owner wants to do and the rent they have agreed. This is your entry, not the app's. Market comparison figures can be entered here to support the conversation, and the app prepares the owner outreach alongside it, listing the facts it used. Where a property has more than one owner on record, all of them are addressed.

Look for: your recorded decision unlocking the next stage, and a "needs verification" line if anything required is still missing. Nothing moves forward until a person has decided.

3

Tenant offer

The app composes the renewal notice using the decision from stage 2 and the tenant contacts it read from RentVine. Where a lease has more than one tenant, the first is addressed directly and the rest are copied. Lease 297 has five tenants, so it is the one to watch here.

You will see three versions of the message: email, resident portal, and text. Only the email version can actually be sent, and only by you from Gmail. The portal and text versions are drafted wording for you to copy if you want them. The app cannot send either one.

Look for: every tenant on the lease appearing, and every figure in the draft tracing back to where it came from.

4

Draft and documents

The app creates the notice as an unsent draft in Gmail and stops. You open Gmail, read it, change anything you want, and send it yourself.

Look for: the draft appearing in your own Gmail drafts folder, addressed and ready, with nothing sent.

The rule that does not change

The app writes drafts. A person sends them. There is no setting anywhere that lets the application email an owner or a tenant on its own, and there is no scheduled or automatic sending of any client message.

If you see "Data too old to act on"

Lease information is only considered current for fifteen minutes. If you leave a lease open on screen for longer than that, the app will stop letting you act on it rather than working from a stale figure. Reload the page and it will read the lease again. This is intentional, and it is the behavior that stops a notice going out with last week's rent in it.

The rest of the app

A walking tour, not a deep dive. The aim is that you recognize each area and know which one to open when something comes up.

Console

Ask a process question in plain English and get an answer with the source it came from. If there is no reliable source, it says so rather than guessing. Your starting page.

Lease Renewal

Everything in section 1. The live desk shows real leases coming up for renewal in the next four months.

Approval Queue

Decisions waiting on a person. Approve, deny, return, or snooze. Approving here records a decision; it does not reach out to anyone by itself.

Communications

Workflow email only, tied to threads the app already knows about. It is not a second inbox and it cannot browse mail generally.

Priority 3 5 minutes

What the app cannot change

You are working in the live application against real data, so the honest question is what happens if you click the wrong thing. The short answer is that the app can read your systems and it can write drafts, and that is the full extent of it. Each line below was checked against the running application on August 7, 2026.

System Can the app change it? How that is enforced
RentVine Read only The part of the app that talks to RentVine is built so it can only ask for information. There is no code in it that can send a change, so this is not a setting that could be switched on by mistake.
The renewal spreadsheet Read only Writing back to the sheet is switched off at two separate controls, both confirmed off on the running system. The team's sheet stays the team's.
Email to owners and tenants Drafts only Five separate ways of sending a client message are all closed off. The app composes a draft into your Gmail and stops there.
Internal notice to the PMI KC team Sends One exception, and it is internal. Filing a Report an issue sends a short notice to a pmikcmetro.com address so we see it. It carries the page and the time, never what you wrote or any client detail.
DotLoop, LeadSimple, QuickBooks, Boom Not connected Not active. Nothing you do in the app reaches any of them.
Text messages Not built No text messaging is active.
So, in practice

Click anything. Open every lease. Record a decision and change it. Compose a draft and delete it. The two things that leave the application are a draft sitting unsent in your own Gmail, and a short internal note to us if you report an issue. Nothing reaches a client, a resident, or an owner without you personally sending it from Gmail.

Context Read once

Known limits, so you do not go looking

These were sent to Dan on August 6 and are repeated here so they are in one place. None of them is a fault. They are things the app does not do yet, and knowing them saves you hunting for a button that is not there.

Replies are not tracked

Once you send a draft from Gmail, the app has no further visibility. It does not know the message went out and it does not read the reply. When an owner answers, someone still records that answer in the app. This is planned work, not built work.

Notifications stay in the app

Attention items appear on the bell. There is no option to have them emailed to you. If that would help, say so and we will add it.

Approvals cannot be relaxed item by item

Each kind of action either needs approval or does not. There is no way yet to grant one person more authority than another. Making Bailey an Admin is the practical answer for now, so her own work does not queue behind Dan.

Market comparison figures are entered by hand

The automatic market data service is connected but not yet switched on, so the app will not currently produce its own market number. Enter comps manually during the test. This is waiting on one account step on our side.

What this means for the test

Three of the four things we are checking can be judged straight away: whether the lease shows up correctly, whether the facts are right, and whether the notice is addressed correctly. The fourth, whether the app's own market number agrees with ours, cannot be judged until the market data service is switched on. It will be recorded as not yet assessed rather than as a pass.

Ongoing Two to four weeks

The next four weeks

The four leases run alongside the normal process rather than replacing it. Work them the way you always would, and record what the app said next to what you decided. The point is a document at the end that shows where the two agreed and where they did not.

Who checks it

Bailey, once a day. Josiah is the escalation if something looks wrong or stops working.

How long

Two to four weeks from today. We stop early if something goes seriously wrong twice for the same reason.

Nothing goes out

Owner notices are composed and reviewed during this window, and held rather than sent. An unsent draft can always be sent later.

What to record for each lease

  • What the app worked out, and what you actually decided.
  • Where the two differed, and by how much. We are treating a gap of more than five percent or fifty dollars, whichever is larger, as a real difference worth discussing.
  • Anything the app raised that you would otherwise have had to spot yourself. This is the most valuable thing you can write down.
  • Anything it got wrong, missed, or made harder than doing it by hand.
Please report the annoyances too

A step that took four clicks when it should have taken one is worth telling us about. So is wording that reads oddly. Those are the things that never make it into a bug report and they are exactly what makes the difference between an app people use and one they work around.
